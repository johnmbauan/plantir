# Garden Expeditions and Creature Bonds

## Product requirements

**Status:** Proposed  
**Product area:** Garden  
**Working name:** Sanctuary Expeditions

## 1. Product summary

The garden becomes a peaceful creature sanctuary built around two connected systems:

1. **Permanent creature bonds:** every achievement creature develops a lasting relationship with the user. Bond progress only moves forward and unlocks personality, stories, animations, and habitat details.
2. **Creature expeditions:** users send teams of one to three unlocked creatures on asynchronous journeys. The creatures always return safely with bond progress and discoveries.

The experience must create anticipation and attachment without creating obligation. It rewards users for returning, but never punishes them for being away.

## 2. Product problem

The existing garden is primarily a visual record of achievements. Unlocking a creature is satisfying, but there is little reason to interact with it again. This limits:

- Emotional attachment to individual creatures.
- Reasons to revisit the garden.
- Long-term value from previously unlocked achievements.
- Opportunities to connect plant-care activity with playful progression.

A traditional virtual-pet system would solve some of these problems but introduce the wrong emotional contract. Hunger, illness, mood decay, or neglected creatures would make the garden feel like another responsibility. That conflicts with the calm experience the product should provide.

## 3. Product vision

The garden should feel like a living collection of companions that remember what the user and their plants have accomplished together.

Users should be able to:

- Unlock original creatures through genuine achievements.
- Learn each creature's personality over time.
- Assemble teams based on personality and expedition talents.
- Send teams away for a period that suits their schedule.
- Return to short stories, keepsakes, and visible sanctuary changes.
- Complete every creature's bond journey without daily attendance.

The guiding rule is:

> Reward return visits. Never punish absence.

## 4. Goals

### 4.1 Primary goals

- Give users a meaningful reason to revisit the garden after unlocking creatures.
- Create emotional attachment to individual creatures.
- Turn achievement collection into long-term, voluntary progression.
- Reinforce useful plant-care behaviour without encouraging false or excessive activity.
- Make the garden feel more like a game while preserving its calm tone.
- Support irregular users as fully as frequent users.

### 4.2 Secondary goals

- Give unlocked creatures an ongoing purpose.
- Make the garden's day, night, habitat, and animation systems more meaningful.
- Create a foundation for future stories and sanctuary customisation.
- Encourage users to explore useful parts of the wider product.

### 4.3 Non-goals

This feature will not include:

- Hunger, thirst, cleanliness, health, or mood meters.
- Illness, death, abandonment, or creatures running away.
- Bond loss, progress decay, or missed-day penalties.
- Daily login rewards or attendance streaks.
- Combat, competitive rankings, breeding, capturing, or trading.
- Paid acceleration, random paid rewards, or purchasable power.
- Functional plant-care features locked behind game progression.
- A complex resource economy at launch.

## 5. Audience and intended play styles

The system must support three overlapping user motivations.

### 5.1 Plant-first users

These users primarily want to monitor their plants. The garden should acknowledge their normal activity automatically and never obstruct core tasks. They can enjoy occasional creature returns without learning a complex game.

### 5.2 Collectors

These users want to unlock every creature, memory, destination, keepsake, and decoration. The journal gives them clear long-term completion goals without deadlines.

### 5.3 Expressive players

These users care about personalities, favourite creatures, team combinations, and sanctuary appearance. They should be able to display preferred decorations and use favourite creatures without being disadvantaged.

No play style should be required to use the core plant-monitoring product.

## 6. Design principles

### 6.1 Absence is neutral

- Creatures remain healthy and content while the user is away.
- Completed expeditions wait indefinitely.
- Rewards never expire.
- Bond progress never decreases.
- Copy must not imply loneliness, disappointment, or neglect.

### 6.2 Real care comes before game optimisation

- The game recognises legitimate plant-care events.
- It must not reward overwatering, unnecessary configuration changes, repeated page opening, or deliberately allowing a plant to become unhealthy.
- Maintaining plant health must be at least as rewarding as recovering from a preventable problem.

### 6.3 Choice changes the story, not basic success

- Every expedition succeeds.
- Team composition changes scenes and bonus discoveries.
- No creature is mandatory for standard routes.
- There is no objectively strongest permanent team.

### 6.4 Depth without busywork

- Interactions are short and intentional.
- Repeated tapping is not a progression strategy.
- There is no inventory maintenance or routine feeding.
- Progress comes from meaningful product use and considered expedition choices.

### 6.5 Original creature identity

- Creatures must have original silhouettes, personalities, names, and habitat roles.
- The system must avoid familiar monster-collecting conventions such as capture devices, elemental type charts, battles, or staged species evolution.
- Visual progression uses behaviour, expressions, accessories, relationships, and habitat details rather than transformation into stronger species.

## 7. Core experience loop

The complete loop is:

1. The user performs genuine plant-care or setup activity.
2. An achievement unlocks a creature.
3. The user meets the creature and learns its first personality detail.
4. The user selects an expedition destination.
5. The user chooses one to three creatures for the team.
6. The expedition progresses without further input.
7. The user returns whenever convenient.
8. The team returns with a story, guaranteed discovery, and bond progress.
9. Higher bonds reveal new behaviour, memories, and personal expeditions.
10. Discoveries visibly enrich the sanctuary and journal.

The loop must remain understandable without a separate tutorial manual.

## 8. Creature unlocking

- Each of the 21 existing garden achievements unlocks one specific creature.
- Unlocking remains permanent.
- Existing users receive every creature corresponding to achievements already earned.
- Newly unlocked creatures arrive through a short, skippable welcome sequence.
- A creature starts at Bond Level 1 immediately.
- The achievement remains visible in the creature's profile as its origin story.
- Hidden achievements continue to use mystery presentation until earned.
- Bond and expedition systems must not reveal the exact conditions of hidden achievements.

The current sanctuary stages continue to expand according to the total number of unlocked creatures.

## 9. Creature identity requirements

Every creature must have:

- An original creature name.
- A one-sentence introductory description.
- A short personality profile.
- A connection to the achievement that unlocked it.
- One primary expedition talent.
- One secondary expedition talent where needed for roster balance.
- A preferred sanctuary habitat.
- Four bond memories beyond its introduction.
- One personal expedition.
- One signature interaction unlocked at maximum bond.
- Distinctive reactions for departure and return.

Creature talents describe ways of exploring rather than combat power. Example talents include:

- Navigation
- Weather sense
- Plant knowledge
- Night sight
- Recovery
- Observation
- Photography
- Communication
- Curiosity
- Resourcefulness

Talent distribution across the 21 creatures must be balanced so that every creature is useful on multiple shared routes and every talent has several possible representatives.

## 10. Creature profile

Selecting an unlocked creature opens a profile containing:

- Name, portrait, and personality.
- Origin achievement and the date it was unlocked.
- Primary and secondary expedition talents.
- Current bond level and progress toward the next level.
- A plain-language explanation of recent bond progress.
- Unlocked bond memories.
- Personal expedition status.
- Expedition history.
- Discovered keepsakes associated with the creature.
- Next bond reward.
- Current expedition status, if travelling.

Selecting the creature directly in the sanctuary also triggers a short contextual animation. Greeting a creature is always available, but repeated greetings do not generate bond progress.

## 11. Bond system

### 11.1 Bond levels

Every creature has five permanent bond levels:

1. **New Arrival:** the creature has joined the sanctuary.
2. **Familiar:** its first preferences and reactions become visible.
3. **Trusted:** a new idle behaviour and a deeper memory unlock.
4. **Companion:** its personal expedition becomes available.
5. **Sanctuary Friend:** its final memory and signature interaction unlock.

The labels may be refined during copy review, but the five-stage structure and meaning must remain stable.

### 11.2 Bond rules

- Bond progress only increases.
- Progress never expires or resets.
- There is no deadline for reaching a level.
- Once a creature is unlocked, it can reach Level 5 through expeditions alone.
- Relevant plant-care activity accelerates progress but is never required after the creature has joined the sanctuary.
- Completing a bond never requires repeating the original achievement, adding extra plants, or owning extra sensors.
- Users cannot complete bonds for creatures they have not unlocked. Many unlocks still require genuine plant-care activity, which usually means at least one sensor.
- There is no login-based bond reward.
- Direct tapping, opening profiles, and repeatedly revisiting pages do not generate progress.
- Bond levels unlock expression and content, not stronger numerical power.

### 11.3 Progress communication

Bond progress should be understandable without exposing a complicated points economy.

- The profile displays a segmented path toward the next level.
- When progress is earned, the user can see its source, such as an expedition, healthy-plant milestone, or relevant care event.
- The interface does not show optimised hourly reward rates.
- The next meaningful unlock is always visible.
- Once Level 5 is reached, the creature remains usable in expeditions and can continue collecting route-specific memories, but no endless level counter is added.

### 11.4 Target pacing

For a user who participates normally:

- Level 2 should follow the first expedition or a small number of relevant actions.
- Level 3 should require several expeditions or a mix of expeditions and genuine care events.
- Level 4 should represent repeated use across multiple weeks.
- Level 5 should feel significant and normally take several weeks after Level 4.

Pacing must not assume daily visits. Long expeditions should provide proportional bond progress so users who check less frequently are not disadvantaged.

## 12. Sources of bond progress

### 12.1 Expedition participation

- Every participating creature receives bond progress when the expedition returns.
- Longer expeditions provide proportionally more progress.
- Appropriate talents can unlock an additional memory or scene, but do not multiply progress enough to create a dominant team.
- A creature can reach maximum bond solely through expedition participation.

### 12.2 Genuine plant-care events

Relevant events can create an additional bond moment for thematically connected creatures. Eligible examples include:

- Creating a plant.
- Registering, linking, or completing meaningful sensor calibration.
- Responding to a genuine watering alert.
- Restoring an offline sensor.
- Recharging a genuinely low battery.
- Maintaining monitored plants in a healthy state.
- Assigning a plant species.
- Adding a meaningful plant photo.
- Reviewing long-term plant history.
- Completing notification or weather setup.

Safeguards are product requirements:

- Reversing and repeating the same setting does not repeatedly award progress.
- Opening the same page repeatedly does not award progress.
- Watering without a legitimate need does not award progress.
- Recovery after dryness must not progress bonds faster than maintaining healthy plants.
- The user must never benefit from disconnecting a sensor, draining a battery, ignoring an alert, or making a plant unhealthy.
- One-time setup achievements are complemented by expedition progress so their creatures remain completable.

### 12.3 Sanctuary milestones

Major collection milestones may create one-time shared celebrations, such as unlocking a new sanctuary stage. These can grant a small amount of progress to all currently unlocked creatures.

They must remain occasional celebrations, not a second progression economy.

## 13. Expedition access and onboarding

### 13.1 First-creature introduction

The first creature unlock starts a short guided experience:

1. Introduce the creature.
2. Explain that creatures take care of themselves.
3. Explain that bonds are permanent.
4. Show the creature profile.
5. Start an immediate local outing.
6. Reveal the return story without a wait.
7. Add the first journal entry.

The introduction is skippable and can be replayed from help.

### 13.2 Full expedition map

- The full map unlocks after the user has three creatures.
- Before that point, single-creature local outings remain available.
- The map must always offer at least one route compatible with the current collection.
- Locked routes explain their broad unlock category without revealing hidden achievement conditions.

## 14. Expedition structure

An expedition consists of:

- A destination.
- A short narrative premise.
- A selectable duration.
- One to three recommended talents.
- A team of one to three creatures.
- A guaranteed discovery category.
- Optional story branches based on team composition.

The user reviews all of this before departure.

### 14.1 Duration choices

Each shared destination offers schedule-oriented choices:

- **Later today:** approximately 8 hours.
- **Tomorrow:** approximately 24 hours.
- **In a few days:** approximately 72 hours.

Requirements:

- Rewards and bond progress scale proportionally with duration.
- Short expeditions must not be substantially more efficient per hour.
- Long expeditions must not be substantially more efficient in a way that makes shorter choices feel wrong.
- Returning after the stated time has no penalty.
- The user cannot pay or watch advertising to accelerate time.
- Personal story expeditions may use a fixed duration when it supports the narrative.

### 14.2 Active expedition limit

- One expedition can be active at a time at launch.
- A returned expedition waits until the user chooses to view it.
- The next expedition becomes available after the return has been welcomed.
- This limit should be revisited only if users feel blocked, not merely to add more activity.

### 14.3 Cancellation

- An active expedition can be cancelled.
- Creatures return immediately and safely.
- A cancelled expedition provides no discovery or bond progress.
- Cancellation has no additional penalty.
- The confirmation clearly states what will and will not be received.

## 15. Team selection and meaningful choice

- Teams contain one to three unlocked creatures.
- Every standard route can begin with any valid team.
- Routes recommend talents instead of specific creatures.
- Matching no talents still produces the guaranteed outcome.
- Matching one or more talents opens additional story branches or discoveries.
- Multiple creature combinations can satisfy each recommendation.
- Favourite creatures remain viable across multiple route categories.

To encourage roster variety without fatigue or punishment:

- The first time a creature visits a destination can reveal a creature-specific observation.
- New team combinations can reveal alternate scenes.
- The interface may recommend creatures that have not visited the destination.
- Frequently used creatures do not become tired, unavailable, or less effective.
- Unused creatures do not become sad.

There must be no permanent optimal team. Talent coverage and route recommendations require review whenever a creature or route is added.

## 16. Expedition destinations

The route catalogue contains six thematic families.

### 16.1 Nearby trails

Introductory journeys focused on learning expedition rules and creature personalities.

### 16.2 Plant-life routes

Journeys involving unusual growth, seeds, species, roots, and sanctuary flora.

### 16.3 Weather routes

Journeys shaped by rain, clouds, wind, sunlight, and seasonal change.

### 16.4 Recovery routes

Stories about restoring habitats, helping lost travellers, or repairing natural spaces. These stories must not imply that real plants should be allowed to suffer.

### 16.5 Nocturnal routes

Peaceful night journeys involving bioluminescence, stars, sounds, and hidden paths. They can be selected at any real-world time.

### 16.6 Archive routes

Journeys that reveal sanctuary history, old maps, creature folklore, and connections between achievements.

Shared routes remain available once unlocked. Recommended routes may change to improve discovery, but content never disappears because the user did not play within a limited period.

## 17. Personal expeditions

Each creature unlocks one personal expedition at Bond Level 4.

A personal expedition:

- Centres the selected creature's personality or origin.
- Allows up to two companions.
- Reveals why the creature chose the sanctuary or what it values.
- Includes at least one meaningful team-dependent variation.
- Grants the creature's most important keepsake.
- Contributes substantially toward Bond Level 5.
- Remains permanently replayable after completion.

Personal expeditions are character stories, not tests of skill or power.

## 18. Expedition outcomes

Expeditions never fail. Outcomes have two layers.

### 18.1 Guaranteed outcome

Every completed expedition provides:

- Bond progress for every participant.
- A short return scene.
- A journal entry.
- At least one new discovery while undiscovered content remains on that route.

### 18.2 Team-dependent outcome

Talent matches and creature combinations may add:

- An alternate story scene.
- A creature-specific observation.
- An additional keepsake.
- A new sanctuary decoration.
- A group interaction.

The user should understand after the reveal how the team influenced the outcome.

There are no failure screens, low-quality loot grades, or messages stating that the user selected the wrong team.

## 19. Discoveries and rewards

The launch version does not use a spendable currency. Rewards are direct, permanent, and cosmetic or narrative.

Reward categories include:

- Creature memories.
- Expedition journal entries.
- Keepsakes.
- Sanctuary decorations.
- New idle behaviours.
- Alternate expressions and poses.
- Group interactions.
- Environmental details.
- Personal expedition chapters.

Reward requirements:

- First completion of a route guarantees something new.
- The system prioritises undiscovered content.
- Duplicate keepsakes are not awarded.
- The route clearly indicates when its main discoveries are complete.
- Replaying a completed route still gives bond progress and can show alternate scenes.
- Random variation can affect presentation but cannot remove the guaranteed reward.
- Rewards never improve sensor readings or other functional product capabilities.

## 20. The expedition journal

The journal is the permanent record of exploration. It includes:

- Destinations discovered.
- Route completion state.
- Expedition stories.
- Teams previously used.
- Creature-specific observations.
- Keepsakes and their origins.
- Bond memories.
- Personal expeditions.
- Sanctuary milestones.
- Gentle hints for undiscovered content.

Missing discoveries use encouraging clues rather than exact checklists when surprise adds value. Completion totals remain available for collectors.

## 21. Sanctuary presentation

The sanctuary should visibly reflect progression.

- Unlocked creatures occupy habitat-appropriate positions.
- Travelling creatures temporarily leave their normal positions.
- Their empty habitat displays a travel marker that opens expedition status.
- The travelling team appears on the expedition map.
- Returning creatures re-enter through a short, skippable animation.
- Bond rewards add expressions, behaviours, small accessories, or habitat details.
- Discovered decorations can be displayed in suitable sanctuary zones.
- Users can choose which unlocked decorations are visible.
- Compatible creatures may perform ambient group scenes.

The existing local day and night cycle remains atmospheric:

- Time of day changes lighting and ambient presentation.
- It does not change expedition rewards.
- It does not require users to visit at particular hours.
- No progression is exclusive to night-time or daytime visits.

## 22. Return experience

When an expedition completes:

- It waits indefinitely.
- Rewards remain intact.
- The user sees a restrained in-product indicator.
- The complete return sequence is shown only when the user chooses to open it.
- The sequence can be skipped without losing its journal entry or rewards.
- The result explains what was guaranteed and what the chosen team additionally discovered.
- Creatures return to their sanctuary positions afterward.

Return copy should express discovery and welcome, never impatience. Suitable framing includes, “Your explorers returned with something unusual.” Unsuitable framing includes, “They have been waiting for you” or “They missed you.”

## 23. Notifications

Supported notification events are:

- A creature has been unlocked.
- An expedition has returned.
- A bond level has increased.
- A personal expedition has become available.

Requirements:

- In-product notifications are concise and can be dismissed.
- External expedition-return notifications require explicit user consent.
- Multiple events are combined when appropriate.
- An unviewed return does not generate repeated reminders.
- No urgency, guilt, countdown, or loss language is used.
- Notification settings can be changed independently of core plant alerts.

## 24. Copy and emotional tone

The tone is:

- Warm
- Curious
- Lightly humorous
- Calm
- Respectful of the user's time

Creatures can have preferences and personality, but must not express emotional dependence on user attendance.

Copy must avoid:

- “Your creature needs you.”
- “Come back before it is too late.”
- “Your bond is fading.”
- “Do not break your streak.”
- “Your creature is lonely.”
- “You missed a reward.”

## 25. Accessibility and comfort

- All interactions are available with keyboard, pointer, and touch input.
- Colour is never the only indicator of bond state, talent matching, or expedition status.
- Creature profiles and expedition choices have readable text equivalents.
- Departure, return, and bond animations respect reduced-motion preferences.
- Important sequences can be skipped and reviewed in the journal.
- Timers show a clear expected return date and time.
- No choice requires rapid input.
- Ambient audio, if added later, is optional and off by default.

## 26. Content scope and sustainability

The original concept risks requiring a unique animation, reward chain, and expedition for every creature before value can be tested. Content must therefore be modular without making creatures feel interchangeable.

### 26.1 Required launch content

- Profiles and talent assignments for all 21 existing creatures.
- Five bond levels for all 21 creatures.
- Four short bond memories per creature.
- One personal expedition per creature.
- At least one signature maximum-bond interaction per creature.
- Six shared destinations, one from each route family.
- At least three narrative variations per shared destination.
- A balanced set of keepsakes and decorations across sanctuary zones.
- The immediate first-creature outing.

### 26.2 Reusable content

Shared destinations, environmental art, return sequences, and decoration families may be reused across stories. Creature-specific writing, reactions, and personal expeditions must remain distinct.

### 26.3 Expansion rules

Future routes should add at least one of:

- A new meaningful talent combination.
- A new relationship between creatures.
- A new sanctuary visual.
- A new story theme.

Routes should not be added solely to increase timer volume or prolong completion.

## 27. Rollout approach

### 27.1 Concept validation

Test the complete loop with a small representative set of creatures and routes:

- Meeting a creature.
- Starting an expedition.
- Understanding talent recommendations.
- Returning after a wait.
- Receiving a discovery.
- Understanding permanent bond progress.

The purpose is to validate comprehension, attachment, and absence of pressure before producing all character content.

### 27.2 Limited release

Release to a subset of users with:

- Existing creature unlocks preserved.
- The introductory outing.
- Shared expeditions.
- Bond progression.
- Journal and return flow.

Observe behaviour and collect direct feedback before broad release.

### 27.3 Full release

Full release requires:

- Complete bond content for all creatures.
- Personal expeditions for all creatures.
- Balanced talent coverage.
- Accessibility review.
- Notification copy review.
- Confirmation that normal plant-care behaviour is not being distorted.

## 28. Success measures

### 28.1 Primary measure

The primary measure is the proportion of eligible users who complete a first expedition and voluntarily begin another expedition within 30 days.

This captures comprehension, return value, and continued interest without requiring daily use.

### 28.2 Supporting measures

- Percentage of eligible users who start their first expedition.
- Percentage who return to reveal the result.
- Number of different creatures used per participating user.
- Percentage of users who inspect bond memories.
- Percentage who complete a personal expedition.
- Return visits to the garden over 30 and 90 days.
- Continued use of the wider plant-monitoring experience after garden visits.
- Qualitative attachment to named creatures.

### 28.3 Guardrail measures

The feature must not produce meaningful increases in:

- Unnecessary watering records.
- Repeated setting changes.
- Artificial sensor disconnection or relinking.
- Notification opt-outs.
- Reports of guilt, pressure, or confusion.
- Abandonment of core plant tasks in favour of garden interactions.

Success must not be claimed from increased clicks alone.

## 29. Product acceptance criteria

The experience is ready for full release when:

- A new user can explain the expedition loop after the introduction.
- Users understand that expeditions cannot fail.
- Users understand that creatures remain well during absence.
- Returning late never changes an outcome.
- Every unlocked creature can reach maximum bond through expeditions, without extra plants or sensors.
- No repeated trivial action provides an efficient progression path.
- No route has a single mandatory or permanently optimal team.
- Every creature is useful on multiple routes.
- Every expedition provides a clear guaranteed outcome.
- Reward content never expires.
- Completed content is recorded permanently in the journal.
- Reduced-motion users can complete every flow comfortably.
- Users can opt out of expedition notifications independently.
- User testing does not reveal language that creates guilt or urgency.

## 30. Decisions intentionally deferred

The following should be decided through concept testing and balance testing rather than assumed:

- Final creature names.
- Final bond-level labels.
- Exact bond progress values.
- Exact number of discoveries per destination.
- Which decorations users can reposition freely.
- Whether more than one simultaneous expedition is desirable after launch.
- Whether optional ambient audio adds enough value.

These decisions may change presentation and pacing, but must not violate the permanent-bond, no-failure, and no-punishment principles.
